Yes — several metals and alloys can be magnetized and later demagnetized. These are called ferromagnetic materials.

The main ones are:

Iron

Nickel

Cobalt

Many steels (depending on composition)

How it works
Inside the metal are tiny magnetic domains.

Normally:

domains point randomly
→ no overall magnetism
When exposed to a magnetic field:

domains align
→ metal becomes magnetic
Then you can demagnetize them by:

heating them

striking/vibrating them

applying alternating magnetic fields

reversing polarity repeatedly

Different Types
1. Soft Magnetic Metals
These magnetize and demagnetize easily.

Examples:

soft iron

silicon steel

ferrites

Used for:

transformers

electromagnets

motors

relays

These are ideal if you want:

ON magnetism
OFF magnetism
Very energy efficient.

2. Hard Magnetic Metals
These stay magnetic for a long time.

Examples:

neodymium magnets

alnico

hardened steel

Used for:

permanent magnets

Harder to demagnetize.

Electromagnets
This is the most useful real-world example.

A coil wrapped around soft iron:

electricity ON
→ magnetic

electricity OFF
→ mostly non-magnetic
That’s how:

maglocks

scrapyard cranes

electric motors

speakers

relays

work.

Shape Memory + Magnetism
There are also exotic materials like:

Magnetocaloric Materials
Change properties under magnetic fields.

Magnetostrictive Metals
Physically deform when magnetized.

Examples:

Terfenol-D

Galfenol

These are used in:

sensors

actuators

sonar systems

Closest Thing to “Programmable Magnetism”
Modern research includes:

Spintronics
Using electron spin states instead of ordinary current.

Magnetic RAM (MRAM)
Stores data magnetically.

Magnetic domain storage
Tiny regions represent information.

This overlaps interestingly with your waveform/memory ideas because:

information = field states
instead of only voltage states.
