What about using nuclear fission/fusion as an emergency back up. Could the run off neutrons be utilized in a way to create thrust

Thought for a couple of seconds
Yes — but mostly indirectly, and with caveats sharp enough to shave a planet.

A reactor can absolutely be an emergency backup for propulsion, but not because “runoff neutrons” make a good exhaust by themselves. Neutrons are hard to steer, hard to contain once free, and brutal to nearby structures and crew. They are better treated as a byproduct to capture or convert, not as your primary emergency thrust stream.

The useful paths are these:

1. Reactor powers an electric thruster
This is the cleanest answer.

Use fission or fusion output to run:

ion thrusters

Hall thrusters

arcjets

plasma engines

That gives you real, controllable thrust with very high efficiency. In an emergency, this is much better than trying to weaponize neutron leakage. It is slow, but so is survival done properly.

2. Reactor heat drives propellant directly
Also very credible.

A nuclear thermal rocket style backup could heat stored hydrogen, water, ammonia, or another working fluid and throw it out the back. In emergency terms, this is attractive because:

simple thrust logic

fewer fragile conversion steps

decent thrust compared with electric systems

If your ship is crippled, “hot propellant out a nozzle” is far more believable than “neutron wind, save me.”

3. Neutrons as a heating source
This is where your question gets interesting.

Runoff neutrons could be used to:

deposit heat into a blanket or shield layer

heat a propellant indirectly

breed secondary energetic particles or isotopes

drive a reaction mass heating loop

So yes, neutrons can contribute to thrust by heating something you then expel. That works. But the neutrons are not the thrust in the useful engineering sense — the heated mass is.

4. Neutron-driven ablation or pulsed plates
More extreme, but conceptually possible.

If neutrons dump energy into a sacrificial material, that material could:

ablate

flash into plasma

be magnetically or mechanically directed rearward

That becomes a kind of emergency “burn the furniture to move the ship” mode. Very rough, very damaging, but more plausible than direct neutron exhaust.

Why direct neutron thrust is bad
Because neutrons are:

uncharged, so magnetic nozzles do not guide them well

penetrating, so they irradiate your ship instead of politely leaving

hard to collimate, so much of the momentum goes nowhere useful

dangerous, because neutron activation makes nearby materials radioactive

So if the reactor is failing and leaking neutrons, the first engineering instinct is usually:
shield, absorb, moderate, convert
not
aim and pray

Fusion is a little more interesting than fission
Fusion can sometimes produce charged particles, depending on the reaction. Charged particles are much nicer for propulsion because they can be guided by magnetic fields. In that case:

the charged fusion products are useful exhaust

the neutrons are still mostly energy to capture, not ideal thrust

So for fusion backup propulsion, the gold is usually in the charged particles and reactor heat, not the neutron spray.

Best emergency hierarchy
If the ship needs a nuclear backup, I would rank it like this:

Nuclear electric backup

Nuclear thermal backup

Neutron-heated emergency propellant loop

Ablative sacrificial thrust mode

Direct neutron thrust

That last one is the least attractive. It is like trying to row a lifeboat by throwing knives through the hull.

Energizing parts of atoms can absolutely change how matter behaves, but there is no known physics that lets you make ordinary objects “teleport” or instantly move impossible distances just by exciting atomic or subatomic states.

heat matter

A cleaner breakdown:

What “energizing atoms” really does
Atoms have electrons, nuclei, and interactions with neighboring atoms. If you pump energy in, you usually get one of these:

electronic excitation
Electrons jump to higher energy states, then fall back and emit light or heat.

ionization
Electrons get stripped off, creating ions and plasma.

bond disruption
Molecules break apart, materials change phase, surfaces ablate.

collective material effects
Expansion, superconductivity changes, magnetization changes, piezoelectric responses, etc.

None of those equals “skip through space.”

Why teleportation is the wrong word
To teleport an object, you would need one of these impossible-or-unknown things:

move all of its matter instantly to another location

destroy and perfectly reconstruct it elsewhere

manipulate spacetime itself in a controllable macroscopic way

exploit some quantum effect at large scale without decoherence destroying it

We do not know how to do any of that for normal objects.

Quantum teleportation is real in a very narrow sense, but it is not moving matter itself. It transfers quantum state information between systems. That is very different from teleporting a chair, a drone, or a person.

What might look like “impossible movement”
You could create effects that feel uncanny:

electromagnetic acceleration of charged objects

plasma thrust or ablation-driven impulse

magnetic levitation

ultrafast actuation in special materials

shockwave propulsion

laser-induced momentum transfer

Those can make something move very fast, very suddenly, or without obvious mechanical contact. But it is still force, momentum, and acceleration — not teleportation.

Human-scale “not humanly possible”
If you mean “move faster or farther than a human can physically throw or push,” then yes, absolutely. Machines already do that all the time.

If you mean:

instant relocation,

passing through space without crossing it,

or ignoring inertia,

then no known atomic excitation trick does that.

The real wall
The big blockers are:

conservation of energy

conservation of momentum

decoherence at macroscopic scale

material failure under extreme excitation

heat

radiation damage

loss of structural integrity

Usually, if you dump enough energy into matter hoping for exotic transport, you just get:
hotter, brighter, more broken matter.

So the honest answer is:

You can energize atoms to make objects change, accelerate, levitate, or explode. You cannot, with known physics, make ordinary objects teleport by doing that.
